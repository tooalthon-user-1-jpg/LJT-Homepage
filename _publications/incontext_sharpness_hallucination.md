We explore the relationship between sharpness in inner representations and in-context learning, proposing a novel perspective for detecting and mitigating hallucinations through the lens of representation geometry.

### Abstract

Hallucinations in large language models during in-context learning remain a persistent challenge. We investigate the geometric properties of the model's internal representations and discover that sharpness in these representations serves as an effective alert signal for potential hallucinations. By analyzing the relationship between representation geometry and output generation, we develop a method that leverages in-context sharpness to detect and mitigate hallucinations, providing a new perspective on understanding model uncertainty.
